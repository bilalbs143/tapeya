# Brand Sponsorship Pitch Deck — Content Guide

**Status:** Content strategy for a PDF/slide deck (not yet built)
**Purpose:** What to put in each slide, why it wins a brand's budget, and what real Tapeya capability backs each claim — so nothing in the deck is generic filler.
**Audience for this doc:** whoever is designing/writing the deck (you, or a designer you brief).
**Related:** `[SPONSORED_BRAND_POSTS.md](./SPONSORED_BRAND_POSTS.md)` (the ad system this deck sells), `[MULTI_VENDOR_MARKETPLACE_PLAN.md](./MULTI_VENDOR_MARKETPLACE_PLAN.md)`, `[OFFICIAL_ACCOUNT_BADGE.md](./OFFICIAL_ACCOUNT_BADGE.md)`

## 0. Ground rule before you build this

**Every number in this deck must be real.** Brand marketing teams see dozens of these decks; a fabricated download count or a made-up testimonial is the fastest way to lose credibility the moment someone asks a follow-up question — and in some markets, misrepresenting audience/reach figures to a paying sponsor is a legal liability, not just a bad look.

Every slide below marks its data needs as either:

- **[REAL — use as-is]** — a genuine product capability, safe to state as fact today.
- **[FILL IN: ...]** — a placeholder that needs your actual number, screenshot, or quote before this deck goes out. Leave it visibly blank in the working draft rather than inventing a plausible-looking figure — an honest "ask us for the current number" beats a fabricated stat.

Section 8 is a single checklist of every `[FILL IN]` in this doc, so you can go gather them in one pass.

---

## 1. Who reads this deck, and what makes them say yes

The reader is a brand's marketing manager or media buyer, usually mid-scroll through several pitches. They are asking three questions, in this order:

1. **Is the audience real and relevant to me?** (reach, demographics, engagement — not vanity metrics)
2. **Can I actually buy something specific, and will I get proof it ran?** (concrete ad formats + measurement, not "we'll figure out a partnership")
3. **Is this team credible enough that my budget is safe with them?** (product polish, team, momentum)

The deck's whole job is to answer those three questions, in that order, as fast as possible. Everything else (mission statements, roadmap slides, "why cricket matters") is supporting cast — keep it short and let the product and the numbers carry the pitch.

**Positioning note — be upfront that Tapeya is early-stage.** Don't try to look bigger or more established than it is; a media buyer who senses inflated positioning stops trusting every other number in the deck. Instead, frame "new" as momentum: lead with the growth trajectory (Slide 6) and the real partners already on board (Slide 10) rather than raw scale, and use the founding-partner angle (Slide 11) to turn early-stage status into an incentive — better rate, more visibility, first-mover story — instead of a liability.

---

## 2. The narrative arc (read this before the slide list)

Don't structure the deck as a feature tour. Structure it as an argument:

1. **There's a big, passionate, underserved audience** (cricket in Pakistan/the region) →
2. **Tapeya is where that audience actually spends time** (product tour proves it's not just an idea) →
3. **Here's the proof people are already using it** (traction) →
4. **Here's exactly what you can buy, and how you'll know it worked** (inventory + measurement — this is the close) →
5. **Here's why you should move now, with us specifically** (packages, credibility, urgency).

Every slide should visibly serve one of those five beats. If a slide doesn't advance the argument, cut it — a tight 12-slide deck outperforms a comprehensive 22-slide one.

## 3. Slide-by-slide content

### Slide 1 — Cover

- Tapeya logo/wordmark, dark background with the brand gold accent (matches the app's own theme — reuse it, don't invent a new palette for the deck).
- One line under the logo: **"Partner with Pakistan's Cricket App"** (or your actual one-line positioning — see Slide 3 for how to write it).
- Subline: "Brand Partnerships 2026" or similar, plus a tagline like "Where the game lives, on and off the field."
- No clutter — this slide's only job is to look premium enough that the reader doesn't bounce before slide 2.

### Slide 2 — The Opportunity (market, not product)

Lead with the market, not Tapeya. This earns the reader's attention before you ask them to care about your app specifically.

- **[FILL IN]** Size of the cricket-following population in your target market(s) (Pakistan primary, UAE secondary — the product already targets both, see §7 targeting note).
- **[FILL IN]** A stat on digital/mobile engagement around cricket in that market (e.g. streaming growth, social conversation volume during major series).
- One sentence framing the gap: most of that audience's cricket *participation* (not just watching) — casual matches, tape-ball games, local tournaments — has **no digital home**. That's the opening line for Slide 3.

Keep this slide to 2–3 stats max, each with a visible source citation. Uncited numbers read as made up even when they're true.

### Slide 3 — What is Tapeya (one clear sentence)

- **One sentence, large type:** e.g. *"Tapeya is where cricket players and fans create, compete, and connect — from a backyard tape-ball match to a full tournament broadcast."*
- Three-pillar breakdown underneath, each a short phrase + icon:
  - **Play** — create and score a match in minutes, no tournament setup required **[REAL]**
  - **Watch** — live streams with real broadcast-style scoring overlays **[REAL]**
  - **Connect** — a cricket-native social feed (reels, posts, profiles, rankings) **[REAL]**
- This is the slide a brand screenshots and forwards internally — it needs to work with zero other context.

### Slide 4 — Product Tour (screenshots, not bullet lists)

This is the slide that proves the app is real and polished, not a pitch-deck concept. Use actual screenshots, not mockup placeholders.

Cover four real, shipped surfaces — one screenshot each, one caption each:


| Feature                                 | What to screenshot                                  | Caption angle                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Quick Match**                         | The create-match flow or a live scoring screen      | "From 'let's play' to a scored match in under a minute — no tournament, no admin overhead."                   |
| **Live Streaming + Broadcast Graphics** | A live stream with the on-screen scoreboard/overlay | "Real broadcast-style overlays — score, batter/bowler stats, wagon wheel — on matches streamed from a phone." |
| **Social Feed / Reels**                 | The feed with a mix of posts and reels              | "A cricket-native feed — highlights, banter, and match moments, not a generic social clone."                  |
| **Tournaments**                         | A tournament bracket/fixture view                   | "Full tournament management — from a street-league to a franchise-style event."                               |


**[REAL]** — all four of these are shipped, working product surfaces as of this session; use current screenshots, not old marketing renders.

### Slide 5 — Audience & Reach

This is the slide brands scrutinize hardest. Be precise, not impressive-sounding.

- **[FILL IN]** Total registered users / MAU / DAU (pick the metric you're proud of, but label it accurately — "registered users" and "monthly active users" are not interchangeable and a media buyer will ask which one you mean).
- **[FILL IN]** Geographic split (Pakistan / UAE / other — the product's ad targeting already supports country-level segmentation, so you likely already have this data in the admin dashboard).
- **[FILL IN]** Age/gender split if you collect it (many apps don't reliably — if you don't have clean data here, say "primarily 16–34" only if you can back it, otherwise omit rather than guess).
- **[FILL IN]** Engagement depth — sessions/week, average session length, or a proxy like matches scored per week / posts per day. Depth metrics are more persuasive to media buyers than raw user counts, because they signal real usage over a downloaded-and-forgotten app.

If you genuinely don't have a number yet, don't skip the slide — replace the stat with the *category* of proof you'll bring to the follow-up call (e.g. "Full audience breakdown available on request — ask about our admin analytics dashboard").

### Slide 6 — Traction & Growth

- Lead with the trajectory, not the size — Tapeya is new, so the growth *rate* is the actual headline stat here, not a raw total. A steep, honest line from a small base is more persuasive to a first-time sponsor than a flat plateau at a bigger number.
- **[FILL IN]** A simple growth chart — downloads or MAU over the last 3–6 months. A rising line is worth more than any single big number; it tells the brand "the audience you're buying into is still growing," which de-risks a first-time buy.
- **[FILL IN]** Any notable spike tied to a real-world cricket moment (a PSL season, a major series) — this doubles as proof the audience is genuinely cricket-engaged, not generic app-store traffic.
- **[FILL IN]** Total matches scored / tournaments run / live streams hosted to date — these are activity proof points that are harder to fake than download counts and show the app is used, not just installed.

### Slide 7 — Why Cricket, Why Now

Short, one slide, mostly to build momentum into the sponsorship-inventory slides. Cover 2–3 of:

- **[FILL IN]** A macro trend citation (T20 league growth, streaming rights values rising, grassroots participation numbers) — cite a source.
- The specific gap Tapeya fills: most cricket sponsorship money goes to broadcast/franchise cricket; almost none reaches the much larger grassroots/amateur layer where Tapeya lives. That's a genuine differentiator — say it plainly.

For each row on the actual slide, use a screenshot or mockup of the placement, not just the description text — brands buy what they can visualize.

### Slide 9 — Targeting & Measurement (the credibility slide)

This slide is what separates "we'll post about you on social media" pitches from a real ad platform, and it's worth its own slide precisely because most grassroots-sports sponsorship pitches *don't* have this — it's a real differentiator.

- **[REAL]** Targeting by country, platform (iOS/Android/Web), and language — say plainly that campaigns can be scoped to exactly the audience segment the brand cares about, not "everyone who opens the app."
- **[REAL]** A locked, board-ready metrics dictionary: impressions (≥50% visible, ≥1s), views (video watch-time or 2s dwell for image/text), clicks, CTR, VTR — these are precise, industry-normal definitions, not hand-wavy engagement scores.
- **[REAL]** An internal admin dashboard showing campaign performance in near-real-time, with per-creative breakdowns.
- **[REAL]** Independently verifiable traffic — Google Analytics (GA4) is live across the app and Cloudflare sits in front of Tapeya's infrastructure, so a brand isn't limited to trusting Tapeya's own numbers; real, third-party traffic and session data can be shared directly on request. This answers the single most common objection an early-stage/grassroots platform gets from a media buyer — "how do we know this traffic is real, not inflated?" — with an actual answer instead of a promise.
- One sentence framing: *"You'll get the same kind of delivery proof you'd expect from a real ad platform — not a screenshot of a post after the fact."*

### Slide 10 — Partners, Ambassador & Social Proof

Tapeya already has real, named partners — this is the single best antidote to "you're too new" skepticism, so it should carry visual weight (logos, not just text).

- **[REAL] Brand Ambassador — Taimoor Mirza, "The Tapeball King."** Officially partnered with Tapeya; a recognized name within the tape-ball cricket community. Use his photo and, ideally, a short direct quote on the slide — a one-line endorsement in his own words lands far better than the title alone. **[FILL IN]** photo + quote.
- **[REAL] Partner Tournaments & Leagues** — TSL, Azadi Cup (BWP), MPL, BSL, PTPL are official league/tournament partners. A logo grid of recognizable local leagues is one of the strongest trust signals a grassroots platform can show a brand — stronger than most raw numbers, because a media buyer can verify it independently. **[FILL IN]** full league names, logos, and one line each on format/region/reach, so each acronym reads as a real, checkable entity rather than a label.
- If you also have a prior brand partnership, testimonial, or organic brand mention that performed well, add it below the partners/ambassador block — one logo + one quote + one number ("X impressions," "X clicks") beats a paragraph of prose. If you don't have one yet, that's fine; the ambassador and league partners above already carry this slide on their own.
- Keep the **"Founding Partner Opportunity"** framing (a preferential rate in exchange for being the flagship brand case study) in your back pocket for brand-specific pitches, but it's no longer the primary angle for this slide now that real partners are confirmed.

### Slide 11 — Sponsorship Packages

Give the reader a menu, not an open-ended "let's talk." Even rough tiers make it easier for a media buyer to bring something concrete back to their own budget approval process.


| Tier                           | Example bundle                                                                                                                   | Positioning                                                                          |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Title / Presenting Partner** | Tournament naming rights + broadcast branding on all matches in that tournament + a guaranteed volume of in-feed sponsored posts | Highest visibility, best for a single flagship activation (e.g. sponsoring a season) |
| **Category Sponsor**           | In-feed sponsored posts/reels campaign + push notification placements, targeted to a specific city/region                        | Mid-tier, good for a product launch or regional push                                 |
| **Marketplace Partner**        | Featured storefront/product placement only                                                                                       | Lowest commitment, good first step for a brand testing the platform                  |


**[FILL IN]** actual pricing or "contact for rate card" — don't publish real PKR/AED numbers in a doc like this without confirming them with whoever owns the commercial terms.

### Slide 12 — Team & Credibility

- **[FILL IN]** Founder/team photos and one-line bios — brands are also evaluating whether this is a team that will still be operating and responsive in 12 months.
- **[FILL IN]** Any notable backing, press coverage, or advisor names, if genuinely true.
- If the team is small/early, don't oversell — lean instead on product polish (Slide 4) and the specificity of the ad-tech (Slide 9) to carry credibility.

### Slide 13 — What's Next (roadmap, brief)

- 2–3 bullets on what's shipping next that's relevant to sponsors specifically (e.g. expanded broadcast overlay branding, additional targeting options, partner-facing reporting dashboards — see the Phase 2/3 roadmap in `SPONSORED_BRAND_POSTS.md` §15 if any of it is genuinely near-term).
- Frame as "get in now while placement is still exclusive/limited," if that's honestly true (e.g. limited ad slots per feed response by design — `slot_every_n` in the actual system — is a real scarcity argument, not a sales trick).

### Slide 14 — Call to Action / Contact

- One clear ask: "Let's schedule a 20-minute walkthrough" or "Reserve your Q[X] slot."
- Contact name, email, phone/WhatsApp.
- Keep the final slide visually calm — no more stats, just the door out.

## 4. Design & tone notes

- **Reuse Tapeya's actual visual identity** — dark background, the brand gold accent, the same typography treatment used in-app. A pitch deck that doesn't visually match the product it's selling undercuts its own credibility.
- **Screenshots over illustrations.** Every claim about the product should be backed by an actual screenshot from the current app, not a stock photo or a generic mockup.
- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs a paragraph to explain, split it into two slides.
- **Numbers get their own visual weight** — don't bury a real stat in a sentence; make it large type with a one-line label underneath.
- Target length: **12–14 slides**. Longer decks lose media buyers who are triaging multiple pitches in one sitting.

## 5. Format-specific slide ideas beyond the core 14

If you want a couple of extra slides once the core deck lands well, these are the next-most-persuasive additions, in priority order:

1. **A single "Sponsor Journey" slide** — a simple before/after or timeline showing what happens from signing to first campaign live (sets expectations, reduces perceived risk of a first-time buyer).
2. **A competitive-context slide** — where Tapeya sits relative to generic social platforms (no cricket context) and to broadcast-only cricket sponsorship (no grassroots reach) — a simple 2x2 or comparison table works well here.
3. **A "Why Now" seasonality slide** — if there's an upcoming cricket season/tournament window, a slide anchoring the pitch to that specific calendar moment creates real urgency instead of generic urgency.

---

## 6. What NOT to include

- **No fabricated statistics, testimonials, or logos.** Beyond the ethical problem, sponsorship contracts often reference the pitch deck's claims — a fabricated number can become a real liability once money is on the table.
- **No engineering jargon.** "Campaign-first architecture with `ad_events` append-only tracking" (real, from the actual design doc) becomes, on a slide, "precise delivery reporting for every campaign." Translate, don't paste technical language into a sales deck.
- **No feature you haven't shipped**, described as if it's live. If you want to pitch something still on the roadmap (Section 3, Slide 13), label it as upcoming — don't let a brand discover in onboarding that a promised placement doesn't exist yet.
- **No walls of text.** If a slide has more than ~25 words outside of a caption, cut it in half.

## 7. Notes specific to Tapeya's actual product (context for whoever writes the deck)

- The product already has a **fully designed** (not yet necessarily fully built — check current implementation status before promising delivery dates) in-feed advertising system with real targeting and metrics — this is the single strongest asset in the whole pitch and should anchor Slide 8 and Slide 9. Most grassroots-sports apps pitching sponsors have nothing this concrete; lead with it.
- Current ad-system targeting scope is **Pakistan and UAE** (`countries: ["PK", "AE"]` in the live design) — if you're pitching a brand outside that scope, say so plainly rather than implying broader reach than what's targetable today.
- The platform spans **three surfaces** — a Laravel API, an Angular admin backoffice (where campaigns are managed), and an Ionic/React mobile app (where the audience actually lives) — this is worth one line on the team/credibility slide as evidence of a properly engineered platform, not a single-developer prototype.
- **Traffic verification is real, not a claim to build later** — GA4 (`app/src/lib/analytics/google.js`) is already wired into the app, and Cloudflare already fronts Tapeya's CDN/infrastructure (`infra/cdn-tapeya/wrangler.toml`). Both can be shared with a sponsor as independent, third-party-verified proof that traffic is genuine — this is a real, already-available asset, not something to promise and build for the first paying sponsor.
- **Official partners are real and named today**: brand ambassador Taimoor Mirza ("The Tapeball King"), and partner tournaments/leagues TSL, Azadi Cup (BWP), MPL, BSL, and PTPL. These give the deck concrete, checkable social proof despite the product being early-stage — lean on them instead of vague "growing community" language.
